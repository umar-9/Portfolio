    Year 12: Project for Student Robotics (https://studentrobotics.org/). Finished in 8th place in the League Table; reached quarter-finals. Led a team of 6 to the quarterfinals and worked on design, electronics, and Python programming. We received a Gold CREST Award and Concept Design Award (Year 12-13) from CSES's TEXPO event. I was involved in programming, electronics and design. As well as research into actuators, motors, and motor control to select components, the use of proportional feedback in code developed my understanding of control systems.     Serving as Technical Lead and Systems Integrator, my hands-on work centered heavily around a Raspberry Pi 4. Because I maintained a systems-level view, I was uniquely positioned to lead integration, avoid bottlenecks, and leverage physical hardware properties to solve software issues.

    Control Systems & Debugging: I added custom Arduino firmware in C to handle hardware interrupts, allowing us to read the encoder continuously without blocking the main control loop. When our motor encoders failed to track distance reliably, I used oscilloscopes to probe the signal lines, diagnosing the root cause as poor connectors, low baud rates, and interrupt data collisions. Prompting an open team discussion, I engineered a system-level fallback: writing Python logic on the Pi to monitor physical motor stall currents alongside microswitch data to reliably detect collisions.

    Vision & Algorithms: I developed a spatial reasoning algorithm for our vision system to confirm zone placement by calculating relative distances and camera angles between the target and adjacent opponent base markers, eliminating false positives caused by relying on absolute loci distance. I grappled heavily with algorithmic robustness to ensure the robot could handle dynamic arena situations.

    Mechanical Design & Team Leadership: I authored our initial mechanism design (Plan A, a complex rack-and-pinion), while delegating a highly reliable pusher mechanism (Plan B) to a teammate. Tracking progress via Gantt charts, I recognized Plan A's tight tolerances were overrunning timelines and made the executive decision to halt Plan A and pivot to Plan B. Once approved by the design lead, I led physical CAD development using Fusion 360.

    Recognising the importance of communication, I ensured subteam issues affecting whole-system deliverables were immediately raised and dynamically reallocated manpower based on the Gantt chart. This proactive approach allowed us to rapidly adapt mounting brackets in the lab when an outdated chassis net was accidentally manufactured.

Year 11: Project for the PA Raspberry Pi Competition, a solution to energy wastage through household lighting. It is able to detect the artificial light coming from your household lighting and provide easy-to-read data to show when you have been wasting energy (either lights on with no-one in the room or when it’s daytime outside). It can be accessed from a web app on any browser, including mobile, which will provide up to date statistics on your total spendings and allow for you to track up to date spendings on household lighting with the help of a Raspberry Pi. The essential information such as times when energy has been used on lighting, monthly spendings on energy and equivalent carbon dioxide emissions can be seen from the homepage. Data from individual days can be looked up and trends over a longer period of time can be tracked, allowing you to make better choices about your light usage.

On the hardware integration side, I innovated a detection system based on the 50Hz AC flicker in artificial light (opting for a custom hardware filter over an FFT). My initial prototype fed a microscopic, high-impedance (2MΩ) photodiode signal into a 741 OpAmp, but the OpAmp's own input impedance created a voltage divider, loading the sensor and destroying the dynamic range. I architected System V2 by introducing an active OpAmp buffer stage with negative feedback, which maximized input impedance and completely eliminated the loading effect. The unattenuated signal passed cleanly through the hardware filter, amplifier, and comparator, stepping down to a 0–3.3V logic level.

I also wrote intermediary Python code that linked the Pi’s GPIO reads to the web interface and queried a sunrise/sunset API to intelligently flag when artificial lights were left on during daylight hours. This project achieved the following:

  • ‘Technical Design Award (Year 10-11)’ award (2023) – Chelmsford Science and Engineering Society TEXPO competition
  • Silver CREST award (2023)
  • National Finalist at PA Raspberry Pi Competition
